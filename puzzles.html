What makes them different

In Hollow Chess every third turn is a board turn, on which you may pick up an empty square and put it down anywhere that touches the rest of the board along an edge. The place it came from becomes a hole. Rooks, bishops and queens cannot cross a hole, kings cannot step into one, and knights jump over. The full rules take a minute to read.

That gives a puzzle two more questions than a normal chess problem. Which square does the king need, and can I take it away? Which line is closed, and can I open it?

Three to try

The king on an island

The white king is cut off from the rest of the board by missing squares. No rook or bishop could ever reach it, but a knight does not care about water. Black has a king, a knight and two moves. Solve this puzzle

Mate in one, with the board

Black to play and mate in one, and no piece move does it. It is a board turn, so Black may lift an empty square instead of moving a piece. One square on this board is the whole puzzle. Solve this puzzle

Two moves, the first one a square

Black to play and mate in two. The first move is a board move: think about which square the white king needs, or which line a black piece is missing. Solve this puzzle

What is in the set

  • 87 mates in one and 66 mates in two, each checked by a solver so there is exactly one way to start.
  • 68 island and peninsula puzzles, where the king is cut off from the board and something has to get across.
  • A rating for every puzzle and one for you. Solve one cleanly and yours goes up, the way a rated game works.
  • A daily puzzle, the same for everyone, with a clock, a streak and a result you can share without spoiling it.

The daily puzzle

One puzzle is chosen each day and changes at midnight UTC. Everyone gets the same position, so you can compare times with a friend. Solve it on consecutive days to build a streak; there are badges at 3, 7, 10, 30, 50 and 100 days.

Getting better at them

Most of the mates come from a handful of patterns: taking away the king’s last escape square, filling a hole so a rook or queen suddenly gives check, and using a knight where sliding pieces cannot reach. The strategy guide covers each of them.
