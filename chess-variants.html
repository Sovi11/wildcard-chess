Chess variants, compared

Every chess variant keeps most of chess and changes one thing. The quickest way to pick one is to ask what it changes: the starting position, the pieces, what you can see, or the board.

Variants that change the start or the goal

Chess960, also called Fischer Random, is the gentlest change: the same game from an unfamiliar position. King of the Hill and Three-check leave the rules alone and add a second way to win, which pulls kings and attacks into the open earlier than normal chess does. All three are good first variants, because nothing you know stops being true.

Variants that change the pieces

Crazyhouse returns captured pieces to play, so material never leaves the board and attacks rarely run out of fuel. Atomic makes each capture destroy its neighbours, which turns a single open line to the king into a win. Both reward calculation over long-term planning.

Variants that change what you know

Fog of War hides everything your pieces cannot see. There is no check, because you may not know you are in it; you win by capturing the king. It is the closest chess gets to poker.

Variants that change the board

This is the smallest group and the one Hollow Chess belongs to.

Duck Chess adds one neutral blocker that both players move. The board keeps its shape, but one square is always closed, and choosing which one is half the game.

Infinite Chess removes the edges. Pieces can travel as far as they like, so there are no corners to trap a king in and endgames look nothing like the ones in books.

Hollow Chess keeps the 64 squares but lets them move. Every third turn of the game is a board turn, and on it you may lift an empty square and set it down anywhere that touches the rest of the board along an edge. The square you lifted leaves a hole. Rooks, bishops and queens cannot cross a hole, kings and pawns cannot step into one, and knights jump over. Over a game the board grows arms, splits into islands and closes up again.

Compared with Duck Chess, the changes are permanent and they add up: a hole stays until someone spends a turn filling it. Compared with Infinite Chess, the board stays small enough to read at a glance. And unlike most variants, checkmate is still the only way to win, so ordinary chess skill carries over directly.

Which one should you try?

  • You want chess with less memorising: Chess960.
  • You want chaos and quick games: Atomic or Crazyhouse.
  • You like hidden information: Fog of War.
  • You like positional play and want a new kind of plan: Hollow Chess. Cutting a bishop’s diagonal or opening a file with the board is a positional idea that does not exist anywhere else.
  • You like puzzles more than games: 5D Chess, or the Hollow Chess puzzles, where the mating move is usually a board move.
